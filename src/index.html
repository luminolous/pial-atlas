ANATOMY, WITH PROVENANCE

A map. Not a single brain.

Pial is an explorable atlas of population anatomy. Cortical parcellations are conventions: their boundaries and the questions they represent differ between schemes. No single scheme is the definitive division of the brain.

Data & attribution

The cortical surfaces and Desikan–Killiany / Destrieux annotations come from FreeSurfer fsaverage5. Deep structures, the brainstem, and the cerebellum are isosurfaces extracted from fsaverage aseg. These assets are modified derivatives of the original data.

Network labels are the Yeo et al. (2011) 7- and 17-network parcellations. Names describe the source scheme; this atlas does not make functional claims about individual regions.

Slices use the ICBM 2009c nonlinear asymmetric T1 population template (Fonov et al.), distributed at 2 mm by TemplateFlow.

Coordinates & representations

fsaverage surface RAS (MNI305) and aseg voxels are transformed into MNI152NLin2009cAsym RAS using an explicit, T1-based affine registration initialized with FreeSurfer’s published MNI305-to-MNI152 transform. The same matrix is applied to all anatomical meshes. This is an approximate population-template alignment, not a subject-specific registration.

Inflated and spherical surfaces preserve fsaverage vertex correspondence but do not retain anatomical positions. The flat map is a derived Lambert projection of the sphere, not an imported FreeSurfer flat patch. Its antipodal seam can overlap and distort; it is for label exploration only. Layout offsets are display-only and can be returned to source positions.

Navigation & label coverage

Desikan–Killiany lobes follow the FreeSurfer lobe mapping. Cingulate cortex and insula are shown separately. Destrieux navigation is derived from the largest vertex overlap with those DK groups; the inspector reports all overlaps. Network labels remain under “Distributed networks” because they cross lobes. The medial wall and unassigned vertices remain explicitly labelled.

aseg’s brainstem and ventral diencephalon are retained as undivided source labels. The atlas does not invent their subdivisions. Tracts, EEG electrodes, and connectomes are reserved for future layers.
